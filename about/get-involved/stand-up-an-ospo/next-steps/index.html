Where to go from here

You don’t have to do everything at once. Here’s a rough sequence that’s worked for campuses standing up an OSPO—skip or reorder whatever doesn’t fit.

In your first few weeks¶

  • Join the conversation: hop into the Network Slack and introduce yourself, and come to an All-Campus Virtual Meetup or coworking session to meet people.

  • Claim a little space on the web: even a single page on ucospo.net tells people you exist and gives you somewhere to point them.

  • Start finding your people: discovery is the heart of an OSPO—begin a mailing list and start surfacing who’s already doing open source around you.

  • Open the Sponsor conversation: identifying an executive Sponsor can be the slowest step, so start it early.

In your first few months¶

  • Hold a first meetup: an in-person first gathering makes the OSPO real and surfaces your community.

  • Map your local partners: reach out to the stakeholders whose work already touches open source.

  • Join a Working Group: plug into a Working Group so you’re connected to the Network’s actual work, not just its meetings.

Staying connected¶

The Network runs on a few simple channels—keep these handy:

  • Slack: the Network workspace for day-to-day conversation.

  • Events: the events calendar for meetups, coworking, and workshops.

  • Working Groups: where the work happens.

When to ask for help¶

Anytime at all! None of this is meant to be solved alone; the Network exists so you don’t have to. Reach out to Laura Langdon (community manager), or just ask in Slack. We’re glad you’re here 🥳.
